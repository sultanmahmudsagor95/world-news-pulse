Eisenkot Signs First Coalition Pact With Lieberman as Israel's Government Talks Enter Decisive Phase

25 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.


Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, in a portrait from June 2026. (Yahav Trudler / Wikimedia Commons — file photo)
Wednesday, 25 November 2026 — Gadi Eisenkot, the former army chief tasked with forming Israel's next government, has signed his first coalition agreement since last month's election, after his Yashar party reached a pact on Tuesday evening with Avigdor Lieberman's Yisrael Beytenu party. The deal is the first alliance between two parties since the 27 October election left no camp with a majority in the 120-seat Knesset.

Yashar said Eisenkot met Lieberman on Tuesday and the two agreed to sign a coalition agreement, with the details kept confidential. Israel's Channel 12 reported that Lieberman could receive the defence portfolio if Eisenkot succeeds in forming a government, with Yisrael Beytenu also in line for the ministry of immigration and absorption. Yashar won 21 seats in the election and Yisrael Beytenu seven.

The agreement was something of a surprise, Reuters reported, because Lieberman has opposed both a government relying on support from Arab parties and a unity government with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Lieberman has said his main aim is to remove Netanyahu from power. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister, and the Knesset was sworn in on 10 November without a new government.

On Wednesday morning, Lieberman opened the door to a government backed by the ultra-Orthodox parties, telling Kan public radio he would allow a government supported by United Torah Judaism and possibly by Ra'am, the Islamist United Arab List. "This will not be a minority government. It will be a government with a Jewish majority," he said. "By the end of next week, we could present a government with a clear Jewish majority." He said negotiations with ultra-Orthodox representatives had continued into the early hours of the morning and that United Torah Judaism lawmakers had "received the green light from their rabbis".

That claim was met with caution. United Torah Judaism publicly denied reports of progress earlier on Tuesday, saying accounts of an agreed draft plan or advances in negotiations did not reflect the situation and that the party had reached no decision, Haaretz reported. The party is meanwhile preparing a formal negotiating team, Ynet reported, including lawmakers Yitzhak Pindrus, Yitzhak Goldknopf and Meir Porush, with party chairman Yaakov Asher overseeing the talks.

Even with Lieberman's seven seats, Eisenkot's bloc holds only 48 of the Knesset's 120 seats, 13 short of a majority. Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 seats, while the Arab-led parties — the Joint List and Ra'am — hold a combined 19. Eisenkot has until 6 December under the 28-day mandate given to him by President Isaac Herzog on 8 November, with the possibility of a 14-day extension. If he fails, Netanyahu would be given a chance to form a government, and if no one succeeds, Israel would face another election next year.

The pact also exposed new strains inside Eisenkot's own camp. The Democrats, the left-wing party led by Yair Golan that won eight seats, announced on Tuesday evening that it was suspending coalition talks with Yashar, citing progress in the unity-government talks with Likud and a deadlock in forming an alternative government. On Wednesday morning the party demanded that Eisenkot take a unity government with Netanyahu off the table, warning that Netanyahu's policies could survive his departure. Party sources told Haaretz the rupture was serious.

Channel 12 reported that Eisenkot's bloc hopes to swear in a government as soon as next Wednesday, 2 December, and to sign agreements with Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad party and the Democrats soon. The bloc is also pressing Shas, the ultra-Orthodox party led by Aryeh Deri, which is still refusing to join, while Bennett has stayed silent. Mansour Abbas, the leader of Ra'am, told Radio Nas that a government of Eisenkot's bloc with the United Arab List and the ultra-Orthodox parties was "the most likely and practical option right now".

Meanwhile, negotiators from Likud and Yashar were due to meet on Wednesday for a fourth round of talks on a possible unity government. The main sticking points are who would lead the government, whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would be included, a rotation of the premiership and a state commission of inquiry into the failures surrounding Hamas's 7 October 2023 attack. No outcome had been reported by the time of publication. Eisenkot told reporters he was confident a government would be formed. "After a 40-minute break, we're getting back to work on this with full vigour, and it will happen," he said, according to the Jerusalem Post's live coverage.

Gaza's health authorities reported on Monday that 1,679 people have been killed and 6,100 injured since the ceasefire began on 10 October. With less than two weeks left on Eisenkot's mandate, the coming days will show whether the first coalition pact can grow into a majority — or whether Israel faces another election.
