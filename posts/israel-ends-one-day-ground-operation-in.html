Israel Ends One-Day Ground Operation in Central Gaza After Families Flee Nuseirat and Bureij

19 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

Displaced Palestinians wait in northern Nuseirat, in the central Gaza Strip, in January 2025. (UNRWA photo, via Wikimedia Commons)

Thursday, 19 November 2026 — Israel's military said on Thursday that a ground operation involving tanks in central Gaza had ended, a day after it began despite a United States-brokered ceasefire. "The operation is completed," the military told the AFP news agency. The defence ministry body that oversees civilian affairs in the Palestinian territories, COGAT, said Palestinians would again be free to move in the targeted areas near the Al-Bureij and Nuseirat refugee camps, and that the Salah al-Din road, Gaza's main north-south highway, had been reopened.

The operation began on Wednesday, when Israeli tanks moved into an area of central Gaza beyond the so-called "Yellow Line", the boundary that has separated areas under Israeli military control from areas under Hamas control since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025. It was the first such ground operation west of the Yellow Line since the ceasefire began. An AFP journalist reported seeing Israeli tanks positioning themselves east of the Nuseirat refugee camp. The military said the operation, which it described as targeted, aimed to dismantle a tunnel it said posed an "immediate and direct threat" to its troops, and said the incursion was a response to what it described as Hamas violations of the ceasefire in the area. A Gaza security source told AFP there had been intense Israeli artillery shelling north of Nuseirat camp.

COGAT told residents of three blocks — 660, 661 and 666 — around Nuseirat and Bureij to stay away from the area while the operation was under way, and closed the Salah al-Din road through the area. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) said its initial analysis showed that more than 18,000 people were staying in the affected area, which covers about 2.5 square kilometres, including around 4,000 people in displacement sites. Several humanitarian facilities are located in the area, providing health care, water, food, protection, sanitation and education, OCHA said. Residents were seen leaving towards the west and south-west, many of them carrying their belongings. "We fear this may be the start of a broader operation and that displacement will begin anew," Mahmoud al-Saafin, a 27-year-old resident of Al-Bureij camp, told AFP.

Hamas denounced the operation as a "blatant violation" of the ceasefire agreement. The incursion came as the Board of Peace, the body set up to oversee the United States-backed Gaza plan, warned in a 12-page report to the United Nations Security Council that the ceasefire was in danger of collapse, accusing both Israel and Hamas of failing to meet their commitments. Around $17 billion has been pledged for Gaza's recovery, but only about $280 million has so far been deployed, and neither the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza nor the planned International Stabilisation Force has yet entered the territory. Hamas rejected the report as "pure disinformation" and appealed to the mediators to intervene.

Three Palestinians were killed in separate Israeli drone strikes in Gaza on Wednesday, according to hospital officials and Gaza's Civil Defence, cited by Al Jazeera. Those deaths were reported separately from the ground operation. More than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, according to an AFP tally based on figures from the Gaza health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the United Nations. Over the same period, the Israeli military says seven of its soldiers have been killed, as well as one civilian contractor.

Israeli Army Radio reported that the operation was coordinated with the United States in advance and was limited to 300 to 400 metres beyond the Yellow Line. Defence Minister Israel Katz said on Wednesday that Israel would keep occupying parts of Gaza, Lebanon and Syria indefinitely until armed groups there were dismantled. The ceasefire, agreed in October 2025, followed two years of war that began with Hamas's attack on Israel on 7 October 2023. Israel and Hamas have regularly accused each other of violating the deal since it took effect.
