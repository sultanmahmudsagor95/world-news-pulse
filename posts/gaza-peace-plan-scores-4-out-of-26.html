Gaza Peace Plan Scores 4 Out of 26 After One Year, Aid Groups Say

20 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

UK-funded tents arrive in Gaza on 1 December 2025, to be distributed by UNICEF to families displaced from their homes. (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office photo, via Wikimedia Commons)

Friday, 20 November 2026 — One year after the United Nations Security Council endorsed a 20-point plan to end the war in Gaza, five leading aid organisations have given the plan a failing grade, saying it has delivered more death, displacement and despair than the peace it promised. The Danish Refugee Council, the Norwegian Refugee Council, Oxfam, Refugees International and Save the Children awarded the plan just four points out of a possible 26 in a humanitarian scorecard published on Thursday, 19 November. The score is down from five points at the six-month mark in April 2026.

The scorecard measures the plan against its own stated goals on civilian protection, humanitarian access, reconstruction and economic development, and freedom of movement and return. It gave humanitarian access a score of zero, unchanged from April, and civilian protection just one point. "One year into the so-called ceasefire, Palestinians are still waiting for the safety and humanitarian relief they were promised," said Jeremy Konyndyk, president of Refugees International. The plan was endorsed by the Security Council in Resolution 2803 on 17 November 2025, a month after the ceasefire took effect in October 2025.

According to the scorecard, at least 1,650 Palestinians have been killed and 5,975 wounded since the ceasefire began, more than double the death toll recorded at the six-month mark. Gaza's health ministry gave a slightly higher figure in its own update on Thursday, reporting 1,662 people killed and 6,007 wounded since the ceasefire, and 74,715 killed since the war began in October 2023. The ministry's figures are considered reliable by the United Nations. Over the same period, the Israeli military says seven of its soldiers have been killed, as well as one civilian contractor.

The scorecard says Israeli control of Gaza has expanded from 58 percent of the territory when the ceasefire began to nearly 70 percent today, even though the plan forbids territorial occupation and requires withdrawal. The aid groups describe the expansion as "displacement by design". In a separate report to the Security Council this week, the Board of Peace, the body set up to oversee the plan, also faulted both Israel and Hamas for failing to meet their commitments, warning that "a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely".

On aid, the scorecard found that an average of just 49 United Nations-coordinated trucks entered Gaza each day in September, against an agreed minimum of 600, and only 2 percent of them carried medical supplies. Kerem Shalom remains the only crossing open for aid, the groups said, with at least four other main entry points still closed. The shortfall has left hospitals short of basic medicines, with deaths reported from treatable injuries and illnesses, according to the scorecard.

More than half of Gaza's population remains in temporary shelters, the groups said, and 94 percent of people needed shelter or household help as of August. Around 1.8 million people are now living in 1,739 displacement sites, up from about one million people in 862 sites a year ago. November rainstorms flooded more than 70,000 tents, and a nine-month-old child died of hypothermia, the scorecard said, as families face a second winter in makeshift shelters.

Recovery has barely begun. The Board of Peace unveiled a $2.45 billion blueprint in late September covering 66 projects, but the groups say it cannot start while the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza has yet to enter the territory and the planned International Stabilisation Force remains undeployed. Around $17 billion has been pledged for Gaza's recovery, but only about $280 million has so far been deployed, according to the Board of Peace report. The report said Hamas agreed in July to a roadmap to surrender its weapons and transfer governance, which Israel rejected, and Defence Minister Israel Katz said this week that Israel would not withdraw until Hamas is dismantled.

The scorecard was published as Israel's military said a one-day ground operation in central Gaza had ended on Thursday, with the Salah al-Din road reopened and families returning to Nuseirat and Bureij. The aid groups said the plan's own benchmarks show it is failing on almost every measure, and called for the ceasefire to be enforced, aid crossings to be opened, and reconstruction to begin.
