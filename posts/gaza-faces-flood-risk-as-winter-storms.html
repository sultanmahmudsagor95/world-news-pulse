Gaza Faces Flood Risk as Winter Storms Arrive, UNICEF Warns

10 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.


Date of news: 10 November 2026

Heavy rain has flooded tens of thousands of tents in Gaza, and the United Nations children's agency UNICEF warned on 10 November 2026 that a damaged water basin in Gaza City could overflow, putting nearby residents at risk of flooding and disease.

UNICEF said Israel had rejected the import of equipment needed to repair the facility, and that the risk was growing as the winter rainy season begins. The warning was made at a press briefing in Geneva on Tuesday, and was reported by Reuters.

The facility at the centre of the warning is the Sheikh Radwan stormwater basin in Gaza City. According to UNICEF spokesperson James Elder, the basin is now contaminated with sewage and is at risk of overflowing after heavy rainfall.


Elder said UNICEF had been working with Israeli authorities and with the Board of Peace, chaired by United States President Donald Trump, for a year on a three million dollar plan to repair the basin. He said COGAT, the Israeli military agency that oversees humanitarian affairs in Gaza, had rejected the project in the past few days.

“Toxic sewage could flow into the streets and tents of Gaza City, forcing children and families from their makeshift homes once more, and grossly increasing the threat of disease. So we're a bit baffled,” Elder told reporters in Geneva.

“Let's do the rehabilitation before this becomes another entirely preventable killer of girls and boys in Gaza,” he said.

According to Elder, COGAT cited security grounds for the decision. Reuters reported that this appeared to refer to strict Israeli rules on the entry of items that could be used for civilian or military purposes by Hamas. COGAT did not immediately respond to a request for comment from Reuters.

The warning came after the first major storm of the season hit Gaza over the weekend. According to Gaza's Government Media Office, about 70,000 tents were flooded within 12 hours, causing about three million dollars in damage. At least five people were killed in storm-related incidents, the office said.

Gaza's Civil Defence said it had received more than 2,100 distress calls by Monday, and warned that more than 2,000 buildings were at risk of collapse. Nearly 400,000 displaced people are sheltering in areas judged highly vulnerable to flooding, according to the same reports.

In a separate incident reported by Reuters, four people, including three small children, were killed when a sand embankment collapsed onto tents at a displacement site southwest of Deir el-Balah in central Gaza. Medics said heavy rain late on Sunday had washed away a rocky sand bank next to the camp. Six other people were injured.

The World Health Organization said at the same Geneva briefing that there were significant risks of communicable disease in Gaza because of flooding, poor sanitation, and insufficient health and water services. WHO spokesperson Christian Lindmeier said nearly one million displaced people across Gaza now faced a medium or high flood risk.

Local authorities say much of Gaza's water infrastructure and vehicles used to pump water were destroyed during the war against Hamas, which displaced most of the territory's more than two million residents. Most of the population now lives in tent camps, according to Reuters.

The flooding has added to wider concerns about shelter and aid as winter approaches. Al Jazeera reported on 10 November that Israel was blocking 60,000 mobile homes and 200,000 tents, and had allowed only 20,000 tents into Gaza. About 85 percent of homes in Gaza have been destroyed or damaged, according to the report.

Gaza's Crossings and Borders Authority said Israel let in no aid trucks or commercial goods on 6 and 7 November, and that five trucks of cooking gas entered the next day. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs said Israel had notified the UN on 1 November that it would suspend shipments from Egypt. Aid groups warned that daily cooked meals could fall from about 600,000 to 330,000 as a result.

A ceasefire brokered by the United States last year called for the eventual reconstruction of Gaza, but Reuters reported that little progress has been made. Negotiators have not agreed on terms for Hamas to disarm or for Israel to withdraw its troops.

UNICEF and the WHO said the immediate priority was to prevent further flooding and disease as more rain is expected. Elder said the repair work should be carried out before the basin overflows, rather than after families are forced to move again.
