Israel's President Condemns 'Despicable' West Bank Settler Attacks as Violence Escalates

24 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

A Palestinian car burned in a settler attack in the West Bank village of Tuba in January 2025. (Mediterranea Saving Humans / Wikimedia Commons — file photo)

 

Monday, 23 November 2026 — Israel's president has condemned a wave of attacks on Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank as "despicable", after suspected Israeli settlers set fire to about a dozen vehicles and several homes near Ramallah over the weekend and violence continued into Monday. The attacks have drawn unusually strong criticism from across Israel's political spectrum and renewed pressure from the United States for action against settler violence.

The arson attacks took place early on Sunday in the villages of Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Masha'al, Kafr Malik and At-Tuwani, residents said. Reporters from the Reuters news agency saw burned-out Palestinian vehicles and walls spray-painted with slogans including "Revenge" and "Death to Arabs". No injuries were reported in the arson attacks, and no one immediately claimed responsibility. Israeli police said they had opened an investigation, and the military condemned the violence, saying forces had been sent to the area.

President Isaac Herzog, speaking on Monday at the ELNET Jerusalem Summit, said there was "no room for ambiguity" about the attacks. "Violence by Jewish extremists against innocent people and communities in the West Bank or anywhere else, is despicable and deserves strong condemnation," he said, calling it "an assault not only on human beings, created in the divine image, but on the ethical way we carry [ourselves], on our history as a people, and on God himself." He said police would "arrest, investigate, and prosecute any perpetrators of violence to the fullest extent of the law".

Gadi Eisenkot, the opposition leader asked to form Israel's next government after the 27 October election, described the arson as "Jewish terrorism". "Extremist groups that set fire to vehicles and homes are undermining regional stability, putting lives at risk and doing grave damage to Israel's national security and foreign relations," he said. He blamed the policies of National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, saying they had "brought the area to the brink of anarchy", and urged the government to use every tool available, including administrative detention. Former prime minister Naftali Bennett also called the attacks "despicable Jewish terrorism" and accused the outgoing government of turning a blind eye.

The violence did not stop after the weekend. On Monday, Palestinian officials said a Palestinian shepherd was shot dead by a settler in the al-Rashayida wilderness east of Bethlehem, and about 30 settler attacks were counted across the West Bank in a single day. About 50 structures were also demolished in the community of Khirbet Samra, according to Palestinian sources. France called the attacks "very serious" and pressed Israel to arrest those responsible, and reports said 10 people had been arrested. The United Nations has warned of a record rise in settler attacks during this year's olive harvest.

Foreign Minister Gideon Saar condemned the attacks by what he called a "handful of extremists", saying they were "against our values and do not represent the law-abiding citizens of Judea and Samaria", the biblical names for the West Bank. Defence Minister Israel Katz said the military had drawn up a plan to hand responsibility for civilian law enforcement involving Israeli settlers in the West Bank to the Israel Police, which he said was better equipped to handle such cases. Mr Ben-Gvir, in response to Mr Eisenkot, said the condemnation amounted to selling out to Arab parties that could support a future government.

The violence has also become an issue in Washington. Hakeem Jeffries, the Democratic leader nominated to become the next speaker of the US House of Representatives, told The New York Times in a Saturday interview that advancing the West Bank Settler Violence Protection and Prevention Act would be the new Congress's most decisive step in its first 100 days. The bill, introduced in April 2025 as H.R. 3045, would restore the sanctions on violent settlers imposed by former president Joe Biden and revoked by President Donald Trump — asset freezes and US entry bans — applying to Israelis and Palestinians alike. Mr Jeffries called the destruction in Gaza "unacceptable, unsustainable and un-American", but did not commit to two other Democratic bills on arms sales or settlements.

Most countries and UN bodies regard Israeli settlements in the West Bank as illegal under international law, a position Israel disputes. More than 500,000 Israeli settlers live in the West Bank alongside about three million Palestinians. With Israel still without a new government nearly a month after the election, and Mr Eisenkot's mandate to form one running until 6 December, pressure over settler violence is now coming from Israel's president, its opposition, European allies and the incoming US Congress leadership at once. Gaza's health authorities reported on Monday that 1,679 people have been killed and 6,100 injured since the ceasefire began on 10 October.
