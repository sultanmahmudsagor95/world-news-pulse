Eisenkot Seals Coalition Deal With Democrats as Ultra-Orthodox Talks Open

27 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, in a portrait from June 2026. (Yahav Trudler / Wikimedia Commons — file photo)

Friday, 27 November 2026 — Gadi Eisenkot, the former army chief tasked with forming Israel's next government, took a second step towards a governing majority on Thursday, when his centrist Yashar party and the left-wing Democrats announced a coalition deal. It is the second coalition agreement for Eisenkot, after his deal with Avigdor Lieberman's right-wing Yisrael Beytenu party on Tuesday, two days after the Democrats suspended their negotiations in protest at Yashar's parallel unity-government talks with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud.

"Gadi Eisenkot, chair of Yashar with Eisenkot, and Yair Golan, chair of the Democrats, have agreed to sign a coalition agreement between the two parties," the two parties said in a joint statement on Thursday. Under the deal, the Democrats will receive four ministerial portfolios, including an expanded national security ministry — currently held by far-right minister Itamar Ben-Gvir in Netanyahu's caretaker government. Haaretz reported that Golan himself is poised to take the national security portfolio.

The Democrats will also receive the labour and welfare, communications, and culture and sports ministries, the party said, with the culture and sports ministry to include a new directorate for democracy. The agreement is a boost for Eisenkot, whose mandate from President Isaac Herzog to form a government runs until 6 December, with the possibility of a 14-day extension. Eisenkot aims to form a government as early as next week without asking for an extension, reports said.

Despite the two signed deals, Eisenkot remains well short of a majority. Yashar, Yisrael Beytenu and the Democrats together hold only 36 of the Knesset's 120 seats — 21, seven and eight respectively — 25 short of the 61 needed to govern. Naftali Bennett's Beyachad party, with 12 seats, is part of Eisenkot's wider bloc but has yet to sign a formal agreement, and Bennett has been pushing for a unity government with Likud instead.

A second breakthrough came on Thursday evening, when the rabbinic leaders of Degel HaTorah, one of the two factions of the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism (UTJ) party, gave the go-ahead for official coalition talks with Eisenkot, Hebrew media reported. Rabbis Dov Lando and Moshe Hillel Hirsch met in Bnei Brak on Thursday evening. According to the KAN public broadcaster, the two sides have agreed in principle to exempt yeshiva students from military service, with tighter oversight of men who do not study but evade conscription. UTJ's negotiating team meets its lawyer on Sunday morning to prepare for the formal talks. Even with UTJ's seven seats, Eisenkot's bloc would hold only 55 seats, still short of a majority.

The divisions inside Eisenkot's camp remain sharp. The Democrats have pushed for an alliance with the two Arab-majority lists, which won a combined 19 seats, while Lieberman has refused to serve in a government relying on Arab parties and has advocated an alliance with the ultra-Orthodox parties instead. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas claimed both his party and UTJ would join an Eisenkot-led government, with the Joint List supporting it from outside — a claim the other parties have not confirmed. Lieberman warned on Thursday that Netanyahu was trying to derail the government's formation, saying he would "drag out negotiations over a 'unity' government until the last moment" and then try to engineer a crisis to force new elections. The Knesset also gave initial approval this week, by 65 votes to 50, to a bill creating a state commission of inquiry into the security failures behind Hamas's 7 October 2023 attack.

In Gaza, the ceasefire came under further strain on Thursday. Defence Minister Israel Katz threatened a renewed offensive, which he called Operation Gideon's Chariots II, that would displace one million Gaza residents southward if Hamas does not disarm voluntarily. "Either [Hamas] willingly disarms and Gaza is demilitarized in accordance with [US] President [Donald] Trump's plan, or the Israel Defense Forces will disarm it," Katz said in a statement. Israeli strikes and gunfire killed at least nine Palestinians across Gaza overnight into Thursday, medics said — a third consecutive night of attacks despite the ceasefire — and Reuters put the day's toll at at least 10. Among the dead was Khaled Abu Nada, a senior Hamas official and Gaza's deputy education minister, killed in a strike on an apartment in Deir al-Balah. Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya called the continued strikes "unacceptable" under the ceasefire deal and said the group was consulting other Palestinian factions on a response. The health ministry's figures were unchanged from Thursday: 1,705 Palestinians killed and 6,163 wounded since the ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025, and 74,772 killed since the war began in October 2023.
