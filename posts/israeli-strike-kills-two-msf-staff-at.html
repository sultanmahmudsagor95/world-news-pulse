Israeli Strike Kills Two MSF Staff at Gaza Clinic as Ceasefire Comes Under Further Strain

28 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

A view of Gaza City, where Friday's strikes took place. (Wikimedia Commons — file photo)

Saturday, 28 November 2026 — An Israeli strike on a clinic run by Doctors Without Borders (MSF) in Gaza City late on Friday killed two of the medical charity's staff, the organisation said, in the latest deadly incident under a ceasefire that has come under growing strain. MSF described the strike as a "direct hit" on one of its clinics and said both of the dead men were watchmen working as security guards at the facility.

"We are devastated and outraged by the killing of two of our colleagues, both watchmen, in an Israeli strike on one of our Gaza clinics tonight," MSF said in a statement on Friday. "Our premises were clearly marked with MSF logos, and their location had been communicated to and was known by the Israeli military," the group said, calling for those responsible to be held accountable. The deaths bring the number of MSF staff killed in Gaza since the war began to 17, the organisation said.

Israel's military confirmed the strike but said the two men were members of Hamas. It named them as Ahmed Sufyan Muhammad Ali Abu Hasira and Moaz Yousef Alitsbach, and said the second man was a commander of an artillery cell in Hamas's military wing. The military described the two as "terrorists posing as humanitarian workers" and said the strike had not targeted a hospital and had caused no damage to medical infrastructure in the area. Palestinian media reported that the strike hit near an MSF clinic close to Al-Shifa Hospital. MSF has repeatedly denied that it employs members of armed groups.

World Health Organization chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called the attack "outrageous and unacceptable". "Those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked," he wrote on social media. Hospitals are protected sites under international law, although medical facilities can lose that protection in certain circumstances. Israel has repeatedly struck hospitals in Gaza during the war, including Nasser Hospital earlier this month, saying Hamas fighters were operating inside them. MSF suspended some of its hospital work in Gaza earlier this year over the presence of gunmen, Reuters reported.

The clinic strike came at the end of a day of Israeli attacks across Gaza. Before dawn on Friday, an Israeli drone strike hit a residential apartment in the Rimal neighbourhood of Gaza City. Hamas said the strike targeted Izz al-Din al-Hayya, the youngest surviving son of its political chief Khalil al-Hayya, and that he and his family had survived. A Gaza security source told the AFP news agency that al-Hayya, his family and the family of his late brother Azzam had survived an assassination attempt. The Israeli military said it was looking into the incident. Four of Khalil al-Hayya's sons have already been killed in Israeli attacks, including one in May and another in a strike targeting Hamas leaders in Doha in September 2025.

Gaza's civil defence agency said Israeli forces killed seven people across the territory on Friday. It said a drone strike west of Rafah in southern Gaza killed three people, with nearby Nasser Hospital confirming it had received three bodies; the Israeli military told AFP it had struck "three terrorists in the area". Another drone strike west of Jabalia refugee camp in northern Gaza killed one person, the agency said, and two more people were killed in a drone strike near Al-Shifa Hospital. Israeli gunfire in the Rafah area killed another person, according to the agency. The Israeli military said it was checking the Jabalia, Al-Shifa and gunfire incidents.

The ceasefire, which took effect on 10 October 2025 after two years of war, has been tested repeatedly in recent days. On Thursday, Defence Minister Israel Katz threatened a renewed offensive, which he called Operation Gideon's Chariots II, that would displace one million Gaza residents southward if Hamas does not disarm voluntarily. Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya called the continued strikes "unacceptable" under the ceasefire deal and said the group was consulting other Palestinian factions on a response. According to the Gaza health ministry, at least 1,705 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took effect, bringing the overall death toll in the war to at least 74,772. The Israeli military says seven of its soldiers and one civilian contractor have been killed in the same period.
