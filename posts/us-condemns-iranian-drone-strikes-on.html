US Condemns Iranian Drone Strikes on Bahrain After Third Attack in Five Days

17 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

File photo: US Navy destroyers of the USS Theodore Roosevelt Carrier Strike Group during an exercise at sea. (US Navy via Wikimedia Commons)

Tuesday, 17 November 2026 — Bahrain said its air defences intercepted Iranian aerial attacks on Tuesday, after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said it had launched drones at a US military radar system at Sheikh Isa Air Base in the kingdom. Explosions were heard over the capital, Manama, and Bahrain urged residents to move to safe locations before later saying the threat had passed. No casualties or damage were reported.

The IRGC said on its Sepah News website that its combat drones had carried out a "precision operation" against a radar system operated by US forces at the base. It said the attack was a response to what it described as repeated violations of Iranian airspace by US military drones. Bahrain's state television said the country's armed forces had intercepted "Iranian aerial attacks in the kingdom's skies". Neither side gave the number of drones involved, and there has been no independent confirmation that the radar was hit.

Tuesday's incident was the third time in five days that Iran has claimed a drone attack on US facilities in Bahrain. On Friday, 13 November, the IRGC said it had attacked a US early-warning radar site; the US Central Command (CENTCOM) said its forces shot down two drones and reported no injuries or damage, rejecting the claim that the radar had been destroyed. On Sunday, 15 November, the IRGC said it had sent six drones against a US air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base; Bahrain said its air defences intercepted and destroyed several of the incoming aircraft, the first such interceptions since September. CENTCOM has not commented on Sunday's or Tuesday's claims, and the reported damage to any radar has not been independently verified.

The United States condemned the attacks on Monday, before Tuesday's incident. Secretary of State Marco Rubio spoke by telephone with Bahrain's Foreign Minister, Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani, and "condemned Iran's attacks on Bahrain and reaffirmed the commitment of the United States to the security of Bahrain and all our Gulf partners", the State Department said. Bahrain is designated a major non-NATO ally of the United States and hosts the headquarters of the US Navy's Fifth Fleet. The two ministers also reaffirmed the long-standing US-Bahrain strategic partnership, including the Comprehensive Security Integration and Prosperity Agreement.

The attacks come as the US military presence in the region grows. The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt entered the US Central Command area on Sunday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington — three carrier strike groups in the region at the same time, an unusually large deployment that officials expect to be temporary. Separately, a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile while leaving the Strait of Hormuz on Monday morning, causing a fire but no casualties, according to The National.

The fighting is part of a wider war that began on 28 February, when the United States and Israel attacked Iran and Iran responded with strikes on Israel and on Gulf states that host US bases. Diplomatic efforts to end the war remain stalled. Iran has proposed a two-day plan under which a blockade would be lifted and an oil waiver granted on the first day and the Strait of Hormuz reopened on the second; the plan was backed by a meeting of the Economic Cooperation Organization in Islamabad on Monday, according to Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Washington has not answered the proposal. Rubio said on 8 November that Iran's demands were "unacceptable", and President Donald Trump said on 12 November that the United States no longer needed a deal. Trump said last Thursday that the US was taking "a little bit longer way, a bit more civilly" at the request of nations he is looking to protect. On Monday, Vice President JD Vance said Trump would not let the conflict become another "forever war".

There were also signs of diplomacy on Tuesday: Araghchi met his Omani counterpart, Badr Al Busaidi, in Muscat for talks on regional tension, maritime security and efforts to revive diplomatic channels, Oman's Foreign Ministry said. In Bahrain, meanwhile, a court sentenced 64 Shi'ite Muslims to life in prison on terrorism charges linked to Iran, the Jerusalem Post reported on Tuesday. For now, the pattern of the past five days — Iranian claims of strikes, Bahraini claims of interceptions, and no confirmed damage — leaves the Gulf braced for further incidents while diplomacy waits for an answer.
