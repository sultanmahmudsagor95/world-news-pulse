Iran Claims Second Drone Strike on US Base in Bahrain as Gulf Tensions Escalate

15 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

Explosions rang out over Bahrain early on Sunday, 15 November 2026, as Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said it had launched a second drone attack in three days on a United States military installation in the Gulf kingdom, and Bahrain issued its first nationwide threat alerts since September.

In a statement carried by Iranian state media on Sunday morning, the IRGC said six drones from its Aerospace Force had targeted a US air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain, in what it called the "second phase of a decisive and crushing response" to US military action. Explosions were heard in the capital, Manama, at around 4:00 a.m. local time (01:00 GMT), with residents reporting a second series of blasts at about 6:30 a.m. Bahrain's Interior Ministry issued two alerts on social media urging residents to remain calm and proceed to the nearest safe place, without naming the threat. Bahrain's military said its air defences had intercepted a number of Iranian projectiles before they reached their targets. No damage or casualties were reported, and neither the US military nor Bahrain had confirmed any hit on the radar installation.

The claim followed a similar one on Friday, when the IRGC said it had targeted a US early-warning radar in Bahrain — the first strike against a Gulf country since early September. The US military's Central Command (CENTCOM) rejected that claim, saying its forces had shot down two Iranian drones heading towards Bahrain and that there had been no casualties or damage. CENTCOM had not commented on Sunday's claim at the time of reporting.

On Saturday, the IRGC said it had shot down a US MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, calling it the 17th such drone it had downed. The United States has not commented on that claim. Iran's navy also said tankers had struck naval mines and caught fire in the eastern Strait of Hormuz, the latest in a series of such incidents over the weekend. The IRGC said in its Sunday statement that the Strait of Hormuz was closed and warned that any further US action would be met with an even harsher response.

The confrontation spread further on Sunday. The Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said it had intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles launched towards the Saudi capital, Riyadh, and three drones in the kingdom's oil-rich Eastern Province. The Houthis, who control most of Yemen's populous areas, have intensified attacks on the Saudi capital in recent days, according to Reuters.

The escalation came as diplomatic signals pointed in different directions. On Friday, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said American economic pressure on a weakened Iran was "so great" that there was no need for further military action, and that the war was, in President Donald Trump's words, "militarily effectively over". Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei accused Rubio of threatening to starve Iranians, singling out his remark about Iranians eventually needing to eat. Reuters, citing three people familiar with the matter, reported that Trump had told senior advisers he might hold off on attacking Iran if Washington faced no new actions it considered provocative from Tehran or Yemen's Ansarallah movement. The White House did not say whether Trump had decided against further strikes.

The wider region remained volatile. Overnight, Israel said it had killed Samir Abu Younis, 53, the commander of Hamas's Rafah Brigade, in a strike in al-Mawasi, west of Khan Younis in southern Gaza. Hamas confirmed his death. Two other people were wounded, according to Gaza's civil defence. He had led the brigade for only about two months, after his predecessor was killed in September.

The war between the United States and Iran began on 28 February 2026, when the US and Israel launched military operations against Iran. CENTCOM said last month that it had helped Gulf countries export 1.5 billion barrels of oil through the Strait of Hormuz since the summer. The strait, one of the world's most important energy routes, has been at the centre of the confrontation, with shipping repeatedly disrupted by mines, drone attacks and interceptions. As of Sunday, the conflicting claims from Tehran and Washington remained unresolved, with no independent confirmation of damage to the radar installation in Bahrain.
