UN Warns 'Lives Are at Stake' as Winter Aid for Gaza Stalls and a Baby Dies of Cold

14 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

 


Flooded tents of displaced Palestinians in Deir el-Balah, Gaza Strip. (Photo: Ashraf Amra / UNRWA, via Wikimedia Commons)

The United Nations and its humanitarian partners issued an urgent appeal on Friday, 13 November 2026, for winter supplies to be allowed into Gaza without further delay, warning that "lives are at stake" after heavy rain flooded thousands of tents sheltering displaced families.

"The devastating impact of the latest rainstorm in Gaza was preventable," the Humanitarian Country Team in the Occupied Palestinian Territory said in a statement. "Children and adults were killed, critical services suspended, and thousands of family shelters flooded, swept away, or torn apart – not by the storm alone, but by policy decisions."

The appeal said that although funding for the winter response had fallen far short of what is needed, more than 150,000 tents bought by humanitarian partners and member states are outside Gaza and ready to be dispatched, most of them in Al Arish in Egypt, just 50 kilometres from Khan Younis. The statement said incoming supplies face multiple approval requirements, limited crossing capacity and unpredictable delivery windows, and it called on the Israeli authorities to speed up their entry, lift restrictions and allow repairs to water and wastewater networks.

The warning came as the first death from cold of the winter was confirmed. Nine-month-old Kinan Ahmed Raqiq died of hypothermia in his family's tent in Khan Younis, in southern Gaza, after temperatures fell overnight. His father, Ahmed Raqiq, told the AFP news agency that he woke to find his son's skin had turned blue and rushed him to Nasser Hospital, but the baby had already died. Gaza's Health Ministry said on Friday the child was declared dead on arrival at Nasser Medical Complex, and a forensic examination confirmed severe hypothermia. Gaza's Civil Defence also confirmed the death.

The United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) has warned that winter conditions put children in Gaza at increased risk of hypothermia and respiratory infections. At least 11 children died from hypothermia last winter, according to the Gaza Ministry of Health, cited by UNICEF. The agency is seeking 97.5 million dollars for its 2026–2027 winter response for 540,000 children and their families.

The baby's death came on the same day that a coalition of 53 countries and organisations, led by the United Kingdom, Qatar and Jordan, called on Israel to allow the immediate entry of shelter, fuel, water, sanitation and health supplies into Gaza. With winter arriving, the group said, "far too many people in Gaza are still without adequate shelter, and risk exposure to hypothermia, respiratory infections, waterborne diseases and other preventable illnesses." It also urged Israel's incoming government to allow rapid humanitarian access and open corridors through Egypt and Jordan.

The World Health Organization warned on Friday of a surge in respiratory illnesses in Gaza, with worse expected after the heavy rains flooded tens of thousands of tents. Acute respiratory infections are 33,000 cases higher than at the same time last year, Dr Reinhilde Van de Weerdt, the WHO representative for the West Bank and Gaza, told reporters in Geneva. Nearly one million displaced people live in flood-exposed areas, and 400,000 of them are in locations considered at extremely high risk, according to the WHO.

The scale of the need is large. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) says 94 percent of Gaza's population urgently needs shelter assistance, and about 65 percent of households are living in tents or makeshift shelters. Some 650,000 children need winter aid, including warm clothing and blankets. UNICEF said on 9 November that flooding had affected more than 106,000 people. OCHA said that as of 8 November, 26,000 households had been affected by the storms. Gaza's Government Media Office said about 70,000 tents were flooded in the first 12 hours of the rain, a figure that has not been independently verified. Five people, including three children, were killed on 8 November when a sandy embankment collapsed onto tents at a shelter site south of Deir el-Balah after the heavy rain.

Israel has said it has allowed large quantities of supplies into Gaza. Responding to criticism from the United States-led Board of Peace, Israeli caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel had allowed in supplies including 91,000 tents, and said that "Hamas alone is responsible for the suffering of the people of Gaza." The UN's Emergency Relief Coordinator, Tom Fletcher, said on 11 November that waiting for a new Israeli government to form was "not an acceptable excuse" for delaying essential winter supplies.

The appeal from the UN and its partners was blunt about what lies ahead. "Winter will last for months," the Humanitarian Country Team said. "Access to longer-lasting shelter must not be made conditional on political progress when people's needs are so urgent. Winter supplies must reach families immediately to prevent further loss of life and protect their dignity. People cannot wait."
