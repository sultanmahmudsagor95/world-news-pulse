US Strikes Iranian Missile Site on Qeshm Island as Gulf Attacks Widen to Kuwait

18 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

A satellite image of Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. US fighter jets struck an Iranian Revolutionary Guard missile launch site on the island on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. (NASA, via Wikimedia Commons)

Wednesday, 18 November 2026 — The United States struck an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday evening, hours after Iran said it had launched drones at a United States military radar base in Bahrain. By Wednesday morning, Iran said it had also targeted a United States base in Kuwait, widening a confrontation that has spread across the Gulf in the past five days.

A United States military official said fighter jets used precision munitions to strike an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) missile launch site on Qeshm Island in self-defence, in response to what the official described as IRGC aggression targeting US personnel and assets. Iran's official IRNA news agency reported five to six explosions on the island on Tuesday evening. Ahmad Nafisi, the deputy governor of Hormozgan Province, was quoted in Iranian media as saying the strike caused no damage to vital infrastructure, residential areas or commercial premises. The extent of any damage to military facilities has not been independently confirmed.

The strike followed the third Iranian claim of a drone attack on US facilities in Bahrain in five days. On Tuesday, the IRGC said its drones had carried out a "precision operation" against a US-operated radar system at Sheikh Isa Air Base, saying it was a response to alleged violations of Iranian airspace by US drones. Bahrain said its air defences intercepted the attack, and explosions were heard over the capital, Manama. The US Central Command (CENTCOM) said the drones were intercepted "without any damage to US equipment or injury to American personnel" and called the IRGC's claim of hitting the radar system "false". The IRGC had made similar claims on Friday, 13 November, and Sunday, 15 November; CENTCOM said it shot down two drones on Friday and reported no injuries or damage.

Early on Wednesday, Iran's regular army said its drones had targeted Patriot air defence systems and drone and missile launch platforms at Ali Al-Salem Air Base in Kuwait. The claim was carried by Iranian state television. Kuwait's military said its air defences were engaging hostile drones and that explosions heard in the country were caused by interceptions. Kuwait reported no casualties or damage. There has been no independent confirmation that any Patriot system or other US equipment was hit, and CENTCOM had not commented specifically on the Kuwait claim. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry condemned what it called repeated Iranian attacks as a "flagrant violation" of its sovereignty. Bahrain's Defence Force said early on Wednesday that its air defences had intercepted several Iranian air strikes, and Bahrain condemned what it described as "criminal attacks". The Gulf Cooperation Council, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates also condemned the attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain.

Shipping in the Strait of Hormuz also came under attack. ADNOC Logistics & Services, the shipping arm of Abu Dhabi's state oil company, said one of its vessels was attacked on Tuesday evening while transiting the strait. One crew member remains unaccounted for and 11 were injured, the company said on Wednesday, adding that all other crew members had been safely evacuated. The company did not name the vessel or say who carried out the attack. Separately, the Saudi-led coalition backing Yemen's government said it had intercepted a Houthi cruise missile aimed at the Riyadh region on Wednesday morning. Al Jazeera's live coverage also reported that two people were killed in separate Israeli drone strikes in Gaza.

President Donald Trump, speaking to CNBC in Manila on Wednesday, reacted to a CNN report about US intelligence assessments of possible Iranian plans involving a dirty bomb or chemical weapons targeting Washington or senior US officials. Trump said the administration was investigating the report and warned that Iran would face unprecedented consequences if it carried out such an attack. The White House said there were no credible threats to the US homeland at the time, and Iran's Foreign Ministry dismissed the allegations as baseless. The report describes an intelligence assessment, not a confirmed Iranian plan.

The fighting is part of a wider war that began on 28 February, when the United States and Israel attacked Iran and Iran responded with strikes on Israel and on Gulf states hosting US bases. Three US carrier strike groups are now in the region, after the USS Theodore Roosevelt joined the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington. CENTCOM's commander, Admiral Brad Cooper, was due to meet Israeli military officials in Israel on Wednesday. Diplomatic efforts to end the war remain stalled: Iran has proposed a two-day plan under which a blockade would be lifted and an oil waiver granted on the first day and the Strait of Hormuz reopened on the second, but Washington has not answered it.
