Netanyahu and Eisenkot Parties End Coalition Talks Without a Deal as Israel's Government Stalemate Continues

23 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, in a portrait from June 2026. (Yahav Trudler / Wikimedia Commons — file photo)

Monday, 23 November 2026 — Talks between the parties of caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot on forming a unity government in Israel ended on Sunday without an agreement, leaving the country without a new government nearly four weeks after an election that gave no camp a majority. Delegations from Netanyahu's Likud party and Eisenkot's Yashar party met for four hours at a hotel in Herzliya in a second round of negotiations after a first meeting the previous Sunday.

In a joint statement after the meeting, the two parties said they had "outlined their positions on a range of substantive issues" and discussed them in detail, adding only that a "follow-up meeting will be held", without giving a date or any further details. The Jerusalem Post reported that the negotiating teams are scheduled to meet again at the Knesset on Monday.

The two sides remain divided on the central questions of who would lead the next government and whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would be part of it, according to Israel's Channel 12 broadcaster. The Yedioth Ahronoth newspaper reported that Likud wanted Ben-Gvir in government, while Yashar insisted on his exclusion. The newspaper Haaretz reported that Yashar's demand to exclude Ben-Gvir was rejected by Likud, and that the question of who would serve as prime minister was not discussed in depth at Sunday's meeting.

Instead, the discussions focused on substantive policy issues. According to Haaretz, the negotiators discussed the establishment of a commission of inquiry into the October 7, 2023 attacks, including the mechanism for appointing its members, and the judicial overhaul laws advanced by the previous Netanyahu government. Sources familiar with the meeting said progress was made on both subjects, but no agreements have yet been reached. The Jerusalem Post reported that the two sides did agree on two principles: recognising Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people and opposing the establishment of a Palestinian state.

The question of a rotation of the premiership was not discussed at Sunday's meeting, Haaretz reported. Former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett, who leads the Together list, had earlier proposed a coalition in which Eisenkot would serve as prime minister first and Netanyahu would succeed him. Al Jazeera reported that Eisenkot has opposed a rotation arrangement, while Netanyahu has reportedly insisted on taking the premiership first. Avigdor Lieberman, leader of Yisrael Beiteinu, has ruled out joining any government that includes Netanyahu. No representative of Bennett's Together party attended Sunday's meeting, Haaretz reported.

Yashar was represented at the talks by lawmakers Chili Tropper and Yoav Horowitz, while Likud was represented by Ofir Katz and Michael Rabello, according to Haaretz. The negotiations followed a meeting last week at which a Likud representative presented Yashar with a document listing ten demands for a unity government.

Eisenkot, a former army chief of staff, was given the first opportunity to form a government by President Isaac Herzog after the election on 27 October. He has until 6 December to do so, with the possibility of a 14-day extension. His bloc won 48 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, 13 short of a majority, while Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats and parties led by Palestinian citizens of Israel won a record 19 seats.

Netanyahu has called for a unity government, saying it is the only way Israel's security can be guaranteed. Such an arrangement would allow a government to be formed without the inclusion of the Arab-led parties. Likud officials, however, told the Jerusalem Post they believe Yashar has little genuine interest in a unity government and is instead seeking to build a minority government supported by the Arab parties. "It's all a show. Yashar does not really want a unity government," Likud sources were quoted as saying.

If the unity talks fail, Al Jazeera reported, Yashar plans to seek a confidence vote in the Knesset for a minority government before Eisenkot's mandate expires. It would rely on outside support from the Arab Joint List, led by Yousef Jabareen, and the United Arab List, led by Mansour Abbas.

Pressure on Eisenkot to end the talks with Likud grew on Monday. Yair Golan, leader of the Democrats party, called on him to halt what he described as "deceitful" negotiations and move forward with forming a government, Haaretz reported. The change bloc has also continued parallel discussions with the ultra-Orthodox parties in an effort to widen the range of possible coalition arrangements.

Until a government is formed, Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister. With the deadline less than two weeks away, the failure of the second round of talks means Israel's political uncertainty continues, and the next meeting at the Knesset will be watched for any sign that the gaps left by Sunday's four hours of discussion can be bridged.
