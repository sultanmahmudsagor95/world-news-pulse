Israeli Strike on Gaza's Nasser Hospital Kills Six, Including a Surgeon

12 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

The emergency entrance of Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis, southern Gaza. (Photo: MSF)

An Israeli air strike hit the Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis in the southern Gaza Strip early on Thursday, 12 November 2026, killing at least six Palestinians, including a surgeon, and wounding at least 20 others, according to Gaza's health ministry and civil defence service.

The strike hit the surgery department on the second floor of the complex's Al Yasin building, causing extensive damage. The facility's director, Raed al-Dahshan, said a missile penetrated the building vertically, passing through several floors, and damaged the surgery department and the intensive care unit. He said he expected the death toll to rise, given the nature of the injuries.

Medics identified the surgeon killed as Dr Rizk Abu Shaira, described by Al Jazeera as a prominent surgeon at the hospital. According to Al Jazeera's correspondent in Gaza, Hind Khoudary, most of those killed and injured were patients, including people who had been scheduled to undergo surgery the following day.

The Israeli military said the strike targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, the commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade, along with other members of the group's military wing, and said the hospital site was being used as a Hamas command centre. It did not provide evidence for the claim, according to the Associated Press.

Hamas condemned the strike as a "war crime" and said the claim that its fighters were in the hospital was an attempt to cover up an attack on a civilian medical facility protected under international law. Gaza's health ministry called the attack a flagrant violation of international law protecting medical facilities, staff and patients, and called on the international community to intervene to protect Gaza's health system.

Nasser Medical Complex is one of the largest hospitals in the Gaza Strip and the only hospital still standing in Khan Younis, serving at least 500,000 people in southern Gaza, according to Al Jazeera. Khoudary said the attack was the seventh time Israel had struck the complex in the past couple of years. A previous strike on 25 August 2025 killed 22 people, including five journalists.

Israel has continued near-daily attacks despite a US-brokered ceasefire that has been in place since October 2025. Since the ceasefire took effect, at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed in Israeli attacks, according to Gaza's health ministry, while Israel says four of its soldiers have been killed in the same period. The overall death toll since 7 October 2023 stands at 74,653, with 176,007 people injured, according to Gaza's health authorities.

The hospital strike followed several deadly attacks on Wednesday, in which seven other Palestinians were killed across Gaza, according to local reports. They included a 38-year-old man killed in an air attack in the Sabra neighbourhood of Gaza City, a man shot dead east of the Bureij camp, and two people killed in a strike on a tent sheltering displaced people in the Sheikh Radwan neighbourhood.

The attacks came as the United Nations made an emergency appeal for waterproof tents, after heavy rains flooded tens of thousands of tents sheltering displaced families. UNICEF said heavy rain had directly affected at least 106,000 people in recent days. Italy announced on Wednesday a new emergency initiative worth 5 million euros (about $5.5 million) to provide immediate assistance to Gaza.

Negotiations over the next stages of the ceasefire have made little progress, with the two sides unable to agree on terms for Hamas to disarm or for Israel to withdraw its troops. Israel currently controls around 60 percent of Gaza's territory.
