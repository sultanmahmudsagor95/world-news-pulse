Eisenkot Gets Mandate to Form Israel's Next Government as New Knesset Is Sworn In

11 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.


 

Date of news: 11 November 2026

Jerusalem — 11 November 2026. Israel's President Isaac Herzog has tasked former military chief Gadi Eisenkot with forming the country's next government, opening a race against the clock to replace Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu after an election that left no bloc with a parliamentary majority. Herzog handed Eisenkot the mandate on Sunday, 8 November, at the President's Residence in Jerusalem, after consultations in which factions totalling 67 members of the incoming Knesset recommended Eisenkot, against 46 for Netanyahu. One faction of seven members declined to recommend either candidate.

The mandate followed the general election of 27 October, in which Netanyahu's right-wing bloc won 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats, while the four Jewish-led parties backing Eisenkot won 48. Arab-led parties won a combined 19 seats — 11 for the Joint List and eight for Ra'am — their strongest showing to date. A government needs the support of at least 61 lawmakers. On Tuesday, 10 November, all 120 members of the 26th Knesset were sworn in, nearly half of them serving for the first time.

Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yashar party and the Israel Defense Forces' 21st chief of staff, has 28 days to assemble a coalition, with a possible 14-day extension. That gives him until 6 December, or 20 December with an extension. If he fails, the mandate is expected to pass to another candidate, and if no one succeeds, Israel could face another election.

Speaking as the new parliament was sworn in, Eisenkot pledged to reverse many of Netanyahu's policies and said he wanted a "national emergency government" as soon as possible. He has set out four conditions for any party or lawmaker seeking to join his government: recognition of Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, acceptance of the values of its Declaration of Independence, support for mandatory military, national or civilian service for all citizens — including communities currently exempt — and recognition of Hamas as a terrorist organisation, with a commitment to dismantling its military and governing capabilities.

His path to 61 seats is narrow. His bloc reaches a majority only with the support of the Arab-led parties — most likely through their votes from outside the government — or with defectors from Netanyahu's bloc. Reports of talks with the ultra-Orthodox parties have also circulated, but Shas said on 8 November that it would not join an Eisenkot-led government, calling such reports false.

Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister while negotiations continue. On the day of the swearing-in he proposed a national unity government, tying the offer to the confrontation with Iran, and warned lawmakers against joining a narrow Eisenkot-led government. Eisenkot rejected the offer the same afternoon. Meanwhile, Eisenkot's bloc has moved to take control of the Knesset's agenda: it nominated Matan Kahana of Yashar for speaker, and 67 lawmakers signed a request to remove the incumbent, Amir Ohana of Likud. The vote is scheduled for Tuesday, 17 November.

In handing over the mandate, Herzog said Israel faced immense challenges, including Iran, advancing the Board of Peace and US President Donald Trump's 20-point plan for Gaza, negotiations with Lebanon, resolving the question of military service, and "healing the wound of October 7th". He urged Eisenkot to build "the best and most stable government possible" and said the country "yearns for it".

For now, the former general — whose son Gal and two nephews were killed in the war in Gaza — has up to six weeks to turn 67 recommendations into a governing majority. Whether he can do so will decide whether Israel's longest-serving prime minister leaves office, or whether the country heads back to the polls.
