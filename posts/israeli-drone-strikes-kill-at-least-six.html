Israeli Drone Strikes Kill at Least Six in Central Gaza as Ceasefire Toll Passes 1,670

21 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

Flooded tents of displaced Palestinians in Deir el-Balah, in the central Gaza Strip. (UNRWA photo by Ashraf Amra — file photo)

Friday, 20 November 2026 — At least six Palestinians were killed in Israeli drone strikes in central Gaza on Friday, Gaza's Civil Defence said, in the latest deadly violence since a United States-brokered ceasefire took effect in October 2025. Al Jazeera, citing medical sources, put the death toll at seven, with the two counts differing over the number killed near Deir el-Balah. The strikes hit three locations in the centre of the territory, a day after Israel ended a brief ground operation in the same area.

Four people were killed when a drone struck south-west of the Bureij refugee camp, in Deir el-Balah, Civil Defence spokesman Mahmoud Bassal told the AFP news agency. Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital and Al-Awda Hospital each confirmed receiving two bodies from the strike, and several other people were wounded. A separate drone strike on a tent housing displaced people at the Al-Munasara camp in Deir el-Balah killed one person, identified by Al Jazeera and AFP as Firas Nabil Aqel, 29, who had been displaced from the Jabalia refugee camp in northern Gaza. A third drone strike north of the Nuseirat refugee camp killed another person. The Turkish news agency Anadolu reported that at least 13 people were wounded in Israeli attacks across Gaza on Friday.

Israel's military said it had targeted multiple military operatives in several strikes. On the Bureij strike, it said it had struck members of the military wing of Hamas "while they were meeting to advance terror attacks", and that measures had been taken to limit harm to civilians. It said it would provide a further update on the strike, but none had appeared by early Saturday. Witnesses told Al Jazeera that the drone in Bureij had struck a group of civilians. The two accounts could not be independently verified.

Demolitions also continued on Friday. Israeli forces carried out demolition operations near areas under their control in the al-Shuja'iya neighbourhood of eastern Gaza City, using explosive-laden drones, and large-scale demolitions in eastern Khan Younis in the south, according to Al Jazeera and Al Mayadeen. By 16 June 2026, an estimated 201,290 structures in Gaza — about 82 percent of all buildings in the territory — had been damaged or destroyed, according to the United Nations Satellite Centre, UNOSAT.

On Saturday, medical sources said at least six more Palestinians were wounded in Israeli attacks across Gaza, including a child and a woman. Two people were wounded by gunfire in Jabalia in the north, a child and a woman were wounded in Al-Kanisa, south-west of Khan Younis, and two people were seriously wounded in a drone strike in Al-Mawasi, west of Khan Younis, according to medical sources cited by TRT World and the Palestinian news agency WAFA. No deaths had been reported on Saturday as of the morning.

Gaza's health ministry said on Saturday that hospitals had received the bodies of nine people killed and 55 wounded over the previous 48 hours. The ministry said 1,671 Palestinians have now been killed and 6,062 wounded since the ceasefire began, and that 851 bodies have been recovered from the rubble in the same period. Since the war began in October 2023, the ministry said, 74,736 Palestinians have been killed and 176,194 wounded. The ministry's figures are considered reliable by the United Nations. The Gaza Government Media Office said on 16 November that it had documented 6,233 ceasefire violations since the truce took effect, killing 1,649 people, wounding 5,969 and detaining 223.

The ceasefire, which took effect in October 2025 after two years of war that began with Hamas's attack on Israel on 7 October 2023, has reduced the scale of fighting but has not stopped deadly incidents. Israel and Hamas have regularly accused each other of violating the deal. The killings on Friday came a day after five aid groups gave the United States-backed plan to end the war in Gaza a score of four out of 26 in a one-year assessment, saying it had delivered little of the safety and relief it promised. Friday was also World Children's Day, when Palestinian prisoner groups said around 350 Palestinian children were being held in Israeli prisons — a claim that has not been independently verified.
