Eisenkot Resolves Rift With Democrats as Israel's Coalition Talks Resume

26 November 2026

Originally published on World News Pulse — read it on the main blog.

Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, in a portrait from June 2026. (Yahav Trudler / Wikimedia Commons — file photo)

Thursday, 26 November 2026 — Gadi Eisenkot, the former army chief tasked with forming Israel's next government, said on Thursday morning that he had resolved a dispute with the left-wing Democrats party, and negotiations between his centre-right Yashar party and the Democrats resumed after a two-day suspension. The Democrats had halted the talks on Tuesday, saying they were frustrated by Yashar's parallel discussions with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud about a possible unity government.

"Marathon discussions aimed at finalizing a coalition agreement will continue this morning," Eisenkot said in a statement on Thursday, according to Reuters. There was no immediate comment from the Democrats or their leader, Yair Golan. The resumption is a boost for Eisenkot, whose presumed bloc holds only 48 of the 120 seats in the Knesset, 13 short of a majority, and for whom the loss of the Democrats' eight seats would have been a serious setback.

The Democrats suspended their talks on Tuesday evening, citing progress in the unity-government talks with Likud and a deadlock in forming an alternative government. On Wednesday morning the party demanded that Eisenkot take a unity government with Netanyahu off the table, warning that Netanyahu's policies could survive his departure. Party sources described the rupture as serious, Haaretz reported.

Haaretz reported on Thursday that the Democrats are now seeking the Education Ministry or the Public Security Ministry, having concluded that they will not receive one of the senior posts — defence, foreign affairs or justice — in the next government. No coalition agreement with the Democrats has been signed yet. Thursday's development is resumed talks, not a deal.

The resumed talks follow the first coalition agreement of the post-election period, signed on Tuesday evening between Yashar and Avigdor Lieberman's Yisrael Beytenu party. Under that deal, Lieberman is in line for three portfolios — defence, justice, and aliyah and integration — according to the Jerusalem Post. Yashar won 21 seats in the 27 October election and Yisrael Beytenu seven. The agreement was something of a surprise, Reuters reported, because Lieberman has opposed both a government relying on support from Arab parties and a unity government with Netanyahu. Lieberman has said his main aim is to remove Netanyahu from power.

Eisenkot's bloc still faces difficult arithmetic. Even with Lieberman's seven seats, it holds only 48 of the Knesset's 120 seats, while Netanyahu's bloc holds 53. The Arab-led parties — the Joint List and Ra'am — hold a combined 19 seats. Eisenkot has until 6 December under the 28-day mandate given to him by President Isaac Herzog on 8 November, with the possibility of a 14-day extension. If he fails, Netanyahu would be given a chance to form a government, and if no one succeeds, Israel would face another election next year.

Meanwhile, negotiators from Likud and Yashar met again on Wednesday for talks on a possible unity government. The main sticking points are who would lead the government, whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would be included, a rotation of the premiership and a state commission of inquiry into the failures surrounding Hamas's 7 October 2023 attack. The Jerusalem Post reported that Netanyahu has been urging the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism party not to rush into a deal with the anti-Netanyahu bloc, although sources said the ultra-Orthodox parties trust the word of Eisenkot and Lieberman. No outcome from the latest round of unity talks had been reported by the time of publication.

In Gaza, the health ministry said on Thursday that the number of Palestinians killed since the ceasefire began on 10 October 2025 had risen to 1,705, with 6,163 wounded. Nineteen Palestinians died in the past 48 hours, the ministry said, 18 in fresh Israeli attacks and one from wounds sustained earlier. The ministry put the total number of Palestinians killed since the war began in October 2023 at 74,772. With less than two weeks left on Eisenkot's mandate, the coming days will show whether the resumed talks with the Democrats can grow into a signed agreement — or whether Israel faces another election.
